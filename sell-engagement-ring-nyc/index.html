First Thing

Nobody Will Ask Why

Rings come across this counter after divorces, after breakups, after upgrades, and after somebody passes away. We have seen all of it and none of it changes the number. You will not be asked to explain yourself, and you will not get a sympathetic speech either. It is a transaction and we keep it that way.

If you would rather do it quietly, come on a weekday afternoon when the shop is slower. You can also text photos first so you know roughly where you stand before you make the trip.

How It Breaks Down

Three Numbers, Not One

Most people expect a single figure for the ring. What we actually do is price three things and add them together, and you see each part.

The centre stone is usually where most of the value sits, and it is graded on its own merits regardless of what the ring looks like.

Side stones — the pavé along the band, a halo around the centre, channel-set baguettes — get valued as a group by total weight. Individually they are small, but on a heavily set ring they add up.

The setting is a piece of gold or platinum with a weight, and it is worth its metal content. A platinum head on a white gold shank is common and the two get weighed separately.

The Big Question

Appraisal vs. Resale Value

This is the single most common source of friction, and it is worth understanding before you walk in, because it is not anybody being dishonest.

An insurance appraisal states replacement value — what it would cost to walk into a retail jeweller tomorrow and buy a comparable ring new, at full retail markup, with the store's overhead and margin baked in. That document exists so your insurer knows what to pay out. It was never a statement of what the ring would fetch if you sold it.

A resale offer is a different measurement entirely. It reflects what the stone and metal are worth on the secondary market today. The gap between those two numbers is normal and it is large. Anyone quoting you close to the appraisal figure is either not buying the ring or not being straight with you.

Paperwork

Certificate or Not

A GIA or IGI report speeds the conversation up because the grading argument is already settled. It is not required. We grade the stone ourselves at the counter either way, and you watch that happen.

If your ring came with paperwork years ago and it is long gone, that is genuinely fine. If the stone turns out to be lab-grown when you were told it was natural — which happens more than people expect — we will tell you plainly rather than quietly adjusting the number downward.

Before You Come

What Actually Helps

Bring the ring and a valid government-issued ID. New York law requires the ID for this kind of transaction, so there is no way around it.

Bring the certificate and the original box if you still have them, particularly for a branded piece — Tiffany, Cartier and similar houses carry a premium above stone value when the piece is verifiably theirs.

Do not get the ring cleaned or polished first. It makes no difference to what we pay, and on an older ring aggressive polishing can actually remove detail worth money.

FAQ

Selling an Engagement Ring, Answered Straight

The questions people actually ask at the counter, including the uncomfortable one about appraisals.

Where can I sell an engagement ring in NYC?
At East Village Buyers, 39 Avenue A in Manhattan's East Village. Walk in during store hours, we value the stone and setting in front of you, and pay cash the same day if you accept. No appointment and nothing gets shipped anywhere.
Why is your offer lower than my insurance appraisal?
An insurance appraisal states replacement value, which is what a retail jeweller would charge to sell you a comparable ring new. A resale offer reflects what the stone and metal are worth on the secondary market. Those are two different measurements and the gap between them is normal and substantial.
Do you value the diamond and the setting separately?
Yes. The centre stone, the side stones as a group, and the metal in the setting are each priced on their own and then added together. You see all three numbers rather than one lump figure.
Can I sell an engagement ring after a divorce?
Yes, and it is one of the more common reasons rings come in. Nobody will ask you about it. Bring the ring and a valid ID and it is handled like any other transaction.
Do you buy rings with small side stones or a halo?
Yes. Pavé bands, halos and channel-set baguettes are valued as a group by total carat weight. Each stone is small on its own, but on a heavily set ring they add up to a real part of the offer.
Should I clean or polish the ring before selling it?
No, and please do not have it professionally polished. It will not raise the offer, and on an older or antique ring heavy polishing can remove original detail that carried value.
What if my diamond turns out to be lab-grown?
We will tell you directly. Lab-grown stones resell for a fraction of a comparable natural diamond, and we would rather have an awkward conversation than quietly mark the number down without explaining why.
What do I need to bring?
The ring and a valid government-issued ID, which New York law requires. The grading certificate and original box help if you still have them, especially on a branded piece, but neither is required.
Do I need an appointment?
Walk-ins are welcome during store hours. If you would prefer a quieter moment, weekday afternoons are the slowest.
