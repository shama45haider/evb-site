Sell an Estate Jewelry Collection in NYC

Bring the whole box. Tangled, unsorted, costume mixed in with the real — that is the normal state of it. We sort it in front of you at 39 Avenue A and explain every pile.

How This Differs

We Take the Whole Box

Selling a single ring and clearing out a lifetime of accumulated jewellery are different jobs, and this page is about the second one.

The usual situation is a shoebox, a jewellery armoire or several drawers holding decades of mixed material: some solid gold, some sterling, a few stones, a great deal of costume, and a handful of things nobody can identify. Almost nobody knows what is in there, and there is no reason you should.

You do not have to pre-sort it, clean it, or work out what is valuable first. Guessing wrong at home is the main way people lose money on an estate lot — good pieces get thrown out as junk far more often than the reverse.

Legal Side

Executors, Probate and Multiple Heirs

A large share of estate jewellery arrives through someone administering an estate rather than an owner selling their own things.

You will need a valid government-issued ID, which New York requires for any transaction of this kind. We do not require probate paperwork or a death certificate to buy, though if you are acting as an executor you may want documentation for the estate's own records, and we can provide a written record of what was purchased.

Where several heirs are involved, it is worth agreeing beforehand who has authority to sell. We are happy for more than one family member to come along, and a detailed itemised record helps when the proceeds have to be divided later.

If a formal valuation is needed for tax or probate purposes rather than a sale, that is a different service from an appraiser, and we will say so rather than pretending otherwise.

At the Counter

We Sort It in Front of You

Nothing goes into a back room. The whole lot gets emptied onto the counter and worked through where you can see it.

Everything lands in one of a few piles as we go: solid precious metal, stones worth looking at closely, pieces worth more intact than melted, and costume. You are told which pile each item is going into and the reason for it as it happens.

The costume conversation is worth expecting in advance. Most estate boxes contain a lot of it, some genuinely lovely, and it typically has little resale value. We will tell you plainly rather than folding it into a vague lump sum, and you are welcome to take it home and try elsewhere.

Practical

What a Full Estate Appointment Looks Like

A single ring takes a few minutes. A full estate lot takes considerably longer, often the better part of an hour, and it should — rushing it is how things get missed.

Text photos to 917-608-8939 before coming in with a large collection so we can set aside proper time. Weekday afternoons are quietest and best for this.

You are under no obligation to sell everything. Plenty of people sell the metal and keep the pieces with sentimental weight, or take an offer on part of the lot and think about the rest. Cherry-picking in either direction is entirely normal.

Payment is cash the same day, for the whole lot or whichever part you decide to sell.

A Word of Caution

Don’t Mail It In

Mail-in buyers advertise heavily to people settling estates, and it is the wrong tool for this job specifically.

An estate lot is irreplaceable in a way a bullion bar is not. There is no second copy of your grandmother's ring. Once it is in a padded envelope you have lost the ability to change your mind, to ask what something is, or to keep the one piece you did not realise mattered to you until you saw it on a counter.

You also lose the sorting conversation, which for a mixed lot is most of the value of doing this in person at all. Somebody in another state deciding privately what is costume and what is not, then sending back a single figure, is not a process you can check.

Come in, watch it happen, ask questions, and take home whatever you decide to keep.

FAQ

Estate Collection Questions

Mostly from executors and families clearing an apartment, which is who this page is for.

Do I need to sort the jewelry before bringing it in?
No, and it is better if you do not. Bring the box exactly as you found it. Pre-sorting at home is the main way people accidentally discard valuable pieces, since a great deal of real gold looks unremarkable next to shiny costume.
Do you buy an entire collection or only the valuable pieces?
We look at everything and tell you what each part is worth. You can sell the whole lot, sell only part of it, or keep whatever you want. There is no requirement to sell everything you brought.
What do I need as an executor of an estate?
A valid government-issued ID is required. Probate paperwork and a death certificate are not needed for us to buy, though you may want an itemised record for the estate's own accounts, which we can provide.
What happens to the costume jewelry?
Most estate boxes contain a good deal of costume and it generally carries little resale value. We will say so directly rather than absorbing it into a vague total, and you are welcome to take it home and try elsewhere.
How long does an estate appointment take?
Often close to an hour for a full collection, against a few minutes for a single piece. Text photos ahead to 917-608-8939 so we can set aside the time. Weekday afternoons are the quietest.
Several family members are inheriting. How does that work?
Agree beforehand who has authority to sell. More than one family member is welcome to come along, and we can provide an itemised record of what was bought, which helps when proceeds are divided.
Can I keep some pieces and sell the rest?
Yes, and most people do. Selling the metal while keeping what has sentimental weight is completely normal, and nobody will push you on it.
Should I mail an inherited collection to an online buyer instead?
We would strongly advise against it for an estate lot specifically. These pieces are irreplaceable, and mailing them removes your ability to change your mind, ask what something is, or keep a piece you did not realise mattered until you saw it.
