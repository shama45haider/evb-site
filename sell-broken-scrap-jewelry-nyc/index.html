The Main Point

Broken Doesn't Mean Worthless

Scrap jewelry is priced on what it's made of, not on whether it still functions as jewelry. A snapped chain, a bent ring, a bracelet with a broken clasp, a pendant that lost its bail, none of that changes the gold or silver content sitting in your hand.

This is the single most common misunderstanding people have walking in the door. They assume damage means a lowball offer, when in practice the metal is worth exactly what the metal is worth, whether the piece is pristine or in pieces.

Common Cases

Single Earrings and Mismatched Sets

A single earring missing its pair is one of the most common things that ends up sitting in a drawer for years because it feels like it's not worth bringing anywhere. It's worth exactly the same, gram for gram, as if you had both.

Same goes for a mismatched set, a bracelet with no matching necklace, or pieces from a set that's been broken up over time by gifts or loss. Each piece is weighed and priced on its own.

A Category People Don't Expect

Dental Gold

Gold crowns, bridges, and fillings are usually a high karat, often 16K or higher, and they test the same as any other piece of gold jewelry. We understand it's not something everyone thinks to bring in, but it has real value and we weigh and test it exactly like a ring or chain.

There's no cleaning or special preparation needed. Bring it as it is.

The Junk Drawer Situation

Bring the Whole Tangle

A ball of chains knotted together, a bag of odds and ends collected over years, costume pieces mixed in with real gold and you're not sure which is which, all of it can come in exactly as it is. We untangle and sort at the counter, separating real metal from costume as we go, so you don't have to guess beforehand.

If some of it turns out to be costume with no metal value, we'll tell you plainly which pieces those are rather than folding everything into one vague numbers.

The Math

Weight, Purity, Live Spot, Same as Anything Else

Every piece gets acid-tested or electronically verified for karat or purity, then weighed in grams, then priced against the live spot price of gold or silver at that moment. Whether it's one bent ring or a full bag of broken pieces, the process is the same for each item.

If a piece has a stone still set in it, we look at that separately. If the stone is missing or it's just bare metal, the number is purely metal weight times purity times spot.

FAQ

Broken & Scrap Jewelry Questions

Almost all of it comes down to one idea: condition doesn't change what the metal is worth.

Does a broken chain sell for less than one in good condition?
No. A snapped chain has the same amount of gold or silver in it as an intact one. We price on metal content, not on whether the piece still functions as jewelry.
Can I sell a single earring if I lost the other one?
Yes, and it's priced exactly the same per gram as if you had the pair. This is one of the most common things people assume isn't worth bringing in, and it is.
Do you buy dental gold like crowns and bridges?
Yes. Dental gold is often a high karat and tests the same as any other gold piece. Bring it as it is, no cleaning needed.
Can I bring in a tangled pile of jewelry I haven't sorted?
Yes, and you should not sort it yourself first. We untangle and sort at the counter, separating real metal from costume pieces as we go, and tell you which is which.
Is a bent or dented ring worth less?
No. Bending or denting doesn't remove any gold, silver, or platinum from the piece, so it doesn't change the weight-based price.
What if some of my jewelry turns out to be costume with no value?
We'll tell you directly which pieces have no metal value rather than lumping everything into a vague total, and you're welcome to take those pieces back.
Do you buy jewelry with missing or damaged clasps?
Yes. A broken clasp doesn't affect the metal content of the piece, so it's priced the same as if the clasp were intact.
