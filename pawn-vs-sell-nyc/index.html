Pawn or Sell? How Pawn Loans Work in New York

Short answer: pawn it if you want the item back and you're sure you can repay soon. Sell it if you don't plan to buy it back. New York lets pawnbrokers charge up to 4% a month, and a loan you don't repay ends with the item being sold. Here is how it works under state law, as published in November 2026.

The Basics

How a Pawn Loan Works

A pawn loan is a loan secured by something you own. You leave the item with a licensed pawnbroker and walk out with cash and a pawn ticket.

1. You leave the item

The pawnbroker holds it as collateral and lends you money against it. In New York City, pawnbrokers are licensed and bonded through the Department of Consumer and Worker Protection.

2. Interest runs monthly

New York allows up to 4% a month, and a part of a month can be charged as a full one. The shop must post its rates where you can see them.

3. You repay, you get it back

Pay back the loan plus interest and the item is yours again. Ask what the payoff will be at each month before you sign.

4. If you don't repay

After the shop has held it for four months, and at least 30 days after mailing you notice, it can sell the item. If the sale brings more than you owe plus allowed costs, the extra is yours.

New York Law

What New York Allows a Pawnbroker to Do

Pawnbrokers, called collateral loan brokers in the statute, are regulated by Article 5 of the New York General Business Law. These are the rules that matter most when you borrow.

New York General Business Law, Article 5, as published in November 2026
RuleWhat the law saysSection
Interest capNo more than 4% per month, or any fraction of a month. The cap applies even where a local law says otherwise.§46

Posted ratesRates must be displayed conspicuously in the shop.§46
Interest limitNo interest or charges for more than 15 months from the date of the loan. If you directly ask for an extension, up to 15 months from the extension.§46
Holding periodThe item can't be sold until it has been in the pawnbroker's possession for four months.§48
Notice of saleWritten notice, mailed to the address you gave when you pawned it, at least 30 days before the sale.§49
How it's soldEach item on its own, at a public auction run by a licensed auctioneer or a commercially reasonable private sale.§48
Money left overAnything above the loan, interest and allowed costs is yours. The pawnbroker must mail you notice within 30 days of the sale, and you have one year to claim it before it goes to the State Comptroller.§50

This is a plain-English summary, not legal advice. Your pawn ticket and the shop's posted rates set the terms of your loan.

Worked Example

What a $1,000 Loan Costs at the 4% Cap

Shops can charge less than the cap, so ask. At the legal maximum, a $1,000 loan costs $40 in interest for each month or part of a month.

4% a month is 48% a year in simple interest. Because a part of a month can be charged as a full month, repaying on day 35 can cost two months of interest. If you don't plan to buy the item back, a loan only delays the same outcome, and the interest and sale costs come out of anything left over. Selling gets you a finished deal today, with nothing to repay.

Before You Sign

Five Things to Ask at the Pawn Counter

A pawn loan can be a sensible tool. These questions make sure you know its real cost.

The monthly rate
Ask the rate and whether a part of a month is charged as a full month. The cap is 4%; a shop may charge less.
The payoff at each month
Ask for the total to redeem at 1, 2 and 4 months, so you see the cost in dollars, not percent.
Other charges
New York law lets auction costs, advertising and lawful extra care charges come out of a sale. Ask what could apply to your item.
Your address
Notice of a sale is mailed to the address you give when you pawn. If you move, tell the shop in writing so any surplus notice reaches you.
The license
NYC pawnbrokers are licensed by the Department of Consumer and Worker Protection. If a shop sells goods before the date on your pawn ticket, you can complain through 311.

The Honest Answer

When a Pawn Loan Makes Sense

A loan is the right tool when you want the item back and the cash need is short.

  • It's a family piece or something you'd never replace
  • You need cash for weeks, not months, and you know where the repayment is coming from
  • The interest for that time is a price you're willing to pay to keep it
  • You've compared the loan amount and payoff at more than one licensed shop

Sell Outright

When Selling Makes More Sense

If you don't plan to buy it back, a loan mostly adds interest and a wait.

  • You don't plan to buy it back
  • You can't see where the repayment would come from in the next few months
  • You want a sale that's finished today, with nothing to repay
  • You have several things to sell at once, like gold, a watch, sneakers or a phone
  • You'd rather trade toward something in our store

FAQ

Pawn or Sell: Questions

What people ask us when they're deciding between a loan and a sale.

What's the most a pawn shop can charge in interest in New York?
4% a month, or any fraction of a month, under General Business Law §46. A shop may charge less, and it must post its rates. A minimum charge of 25 cents a month is allowed.
How long do I have before a pawnbroker can sell my item?
In New York, the item can't be sold until the pawnbroker has held it for four months, and written notice must be mailed to you at least 30 days before the sale. Your pawn ticket shows your loan's actual terms.
What happens if I don't pay back a pawn loan?
After the four-month hold and the 30-day notice, the pawnbroker can sell the item at a public auction or a commercially reasonable private sale. If it brings more than the loan, interest and allowed costs, the surplus is yours: the broker must mail you notice within 30 days, and you have one year to claim it.
Is it better to pawn or sell gold?
If you want the piece back and can repay quickly, a loan lets you keep it. If you don't, selling avoids interest. Either way, check the gold price per gram first so you know what the metal alone is worth.
How much does a pawn loan cost?
At New York's 4% monthly cap, a $1,000 loan costs $40 a month in interest: $1,160 to redeem after four months, or $1,480 after a year. Shops can charge less, so ask for the payoff in dollars before you sign.
