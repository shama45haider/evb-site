What We Buy

Sports Models, Dress Models, Vintage

Every Rolex is a Rolex, but the market doesn't treat them the same. The steel sports models have the deepest demand; precious-metal pieces carry their gold as a floor; vintage is its own market where original parts decide almost everything.

Submariner & GMT-Master II

The two most-traded steel Rolexes. Date and no-date Submariners, and GMTs in every bezel: Pepsi, Batman, Root Beer and the left-handed Sprite. Steel pieces with the full set lead.

  • GMT-Master II
  • Sea-Dweller

Cosmograph Daytona

The chronograph. Steel, two-tone, gold and platinum, on bracelet or Oysterflex. Dial color and bezel material move the number more than on any other model.

  • Steel
  • Gold
  • Oysterflex

Datejust & Oyster Perpetual

The everyday Rolex: 26 to 41 mm, Jubilee or Oyster bracelet, fluted or smooth bezel. Two-tone Datejusts are some of the most common watches we see, and the factory diamond-dial versions hold up well.

  • Datejust 36
  • Datejust 41
  • Oyster Perpetual

Day-Date & Sky-Dweller

The Day-Date only comes in gold or platinum, so the case and bracelet are worth their metal before brand demand is counted. The President bracelet and an original dial are what collectors check first.

Pricing

What Moves a Rolex Offer

Two Datejusts that look alike across a room can be far apart in value. Here is what decides it.

Reference and metal
The reference number tells us the model, size, metal and generation. A current steel Submariner and a 1990s two-tone Datejust are different markets.
Box and papers
The warranty card ties the serial number to the original sale. A full set (box, card, booklets, tags) brings more than the watch alone, but a watch without papers still sells.
Aftermarket diamonds
Stones, bezels or dials added after the watch left Rolex usually lower its value to the market, not raise it. Factory diamond pieces are a different story.
Polishing
Every polish takes metal off. Sharp lug edges and crisp case lines are worth more than a mirror shine on a softened case.
Running and service
A watch that stopped, runs fast or has a foggy crystal needs service, and the cost of that service comes off the offer. It doesn't stop us from buying it.
Bracelet
Older bracelets stretch between the links. Missing links shrink the size range the watch can sell to, so bring every spare link you have.

Not sure which Rolex you have? The reference is engraved on the case between the lugs at 12 o'clock and printed on the warranty card. Text it with photos to 917-608-8939.

Authentication

How We Check a Rolex

Counterfeit Rolexes are better than they used to be, so no single detail settles it. We look at the whole watch, in front of you, and tell you what we see.

  • Serial number on the rehaut (the inner ring at 6 o'clock) on newer watches, between the lugs on older ones
  • The laser-etched crown in the crystal at 6 o'clock, visible under a loupe
  • Cyclops magnification over the date, dial printing and lume application
  • Clasp stamps, end links and how the bracelet fits the case
  • Whether the card and the serial on the watch match

Get Ready

Before You Bring It In

Don't have it polished or "cleaned up" before you sell. Bring it as it is.

  • Bring the box, warranty card, booklets and any receipts
  • Bring Rolex service papers if it has been serviced
  • Bring every spare bracelet link
  • Bring a valid government-issued ID; New York law requires it for every sale

FAQ

Rolex Questions

What sellers ask us most at the counter.

Do you buy a Rolex without the box and papers?
Yes. Papers add to the offer, but a watch that passes authentication sells without them. Plenty of the Rolexes we buy were gifted or inherited with nothing else.
Do you buy a Rolex that doesn't run?
Yes. A stopped Rolex still has real value. We factor the cost of a service into the offer, so bring it in as it is.
My Rolex has diamonds that were added later. Will you buy it?
Yes. Most of the market values it as a modified watch, usually below a stock one, and the added stones count for less than people expect. We'll show you how we get to the number.
Do you buy two-tone and gold Rolexes?
Yes. Two-tone Datejusts are among the most common watches we see. Solid gold models like the Day-Date are worth at least the gold in the case and bracelet, before the brand is counted.
Do you buy vintage Rolex?
Yes. On older watches, original dials, hands and bezel inserts matter most. Parts replaced during a service lower the value to collectors, so tell us what you know about its history.
Can I get a quote before coming in?
Yes. Text photos of the dial, the side of the case, the clasp and the warranty card if you have it to 917-608-8939 for a rough range. Final offers are made in person.
