Good To Know

You Don’t Need to Know Anything About Diamonds

Most people who sell us a diamond have never done it before, and a lot of them arrive convinced they’re about to get taken advantage of. Fair enough — the diamond trade has earned that reputation. So we do it the opposite way: the stone stays on the counter where you can see it, we say out loud what we’re looking at through the loupe, and you hear the reasoning behind the number before the number ever comes up.

If you walk out without selling, that’s a completely normal outcome and it costs you nothing. Plenty of people come in just to find out what they actually have.

What We Buy

Diamonds We See Every Week

Engagement rings. Covered in depth on our engagement ring page. The single most common piece that comes through the door. No explanation required, and nobody here is going to ask for one.

Loose diamonds. Easier to grade accurately than set stones, since nothing is hidden underneath a setting.

Inherited and estate jewelry. Older cuts — European, Old Mine, transitional — are graded differently than modern brilliants, and we price them on their own terms.

Diamond tennis bracelets, studs, and pendants. Valued on total carat weight plus the quality of the individual stones, not just the piece overall.

Branded pieces. Tiffany, Cartier, Van Cleef and similar houses carry a premium above stone value when the piece is genuine. Original boxes and papers help but aren’t required.

Damaged or dated settings. A chipped setting or an out-of-style mount doesn’t hurt the diamond itself. The stone is usually where most of the value sits.

How We Price It

What Actually Decides Your Number

Diamonds don’t have a spot price the way gold and silver do. Two stones of the same carat weight can be worth wildly different amounts, so every piece has to be looked at on its own.

Carat weight matters, but not on a straight line — value climbs sharply at the round numbers buyers ask for, so a stone just under 1.00ct is worth noticeably less than one just over.

Cut is the one most people underestimate. A well-cut stone returns light far better than a poorly cut one of identical weight and color, and that difference is visible across the counter.

Color and clarity set the grade range. We’ll show you what we’re seeing under magnification rather than just naming a letter and expecting you to take our word for it.

Natural or lab-grown changes the answer completely. Lab-grown stones resell for a fraction of a comparable natural diamond, and we test rather than assume — sometimes that means telling someone their stone isn’t what they were originally told it was.

The setting gets valued separately, on its gold or platinum content, then added to the stone value.

In Person vs. Mail-In

Why Not Just Mail It Somewhere?

Mail-in diamond buyers advertise heavily and the pitch sounds easy. The part that gets left out is that you ship your ring to someone you’ve never met, wait, and then receive an offer once they already have the stone in hand. If the number comes back lower than you expected, your leverage at that point is essentially zero.

Selling in person removes that entire problem. You keep your ring the whole time, you watch the evaluation happen, and you can end the conversation and walk out with it at any point. If we’re not the right buyer for your piece, we’ll tell you that too.

FAQ

Selling a Diamond in NYC, Answered Straight

The questions we get asked at the counter most often, including the ones people feel awkward asking.

Where can I sell my diamond ring in NYC?
You can sell a diamond ring in person at East Village Buyers, 39 Avenue A in Manhattan's East Village. Walk in during store hours, we examine the stone in front of you, explain what we are seeing, and pay cash the same day if you accept the offer. No appointment needed and no shipping your ring off to a stranger.
Where can I sell a diamond without a GIA certificate?
Right here. A certificate makes the conversation faster, but it is not required. We grade the stone ourselves at the counter using a loupe and testing equipment, and walk you through the color, clarity, cut, and carat weight before we ever mention a number.
How much is my diamond ring actually worth?
It depends on the four Cs, carat weight, cut, color, and clarity, plus the setting metal and whether the stone is natural or lab-grown. Two rings that look identical across a counter can be worth very different amounts, which is why we examine each one individually instead of quoting from a photo.
Do you buy lab-grown diamonds?
We do, though it is worth knowing upfront that lab-grown stones resell for considerably less than natural diamonds of the same size and grade. We will tell you honestly which one you have, including in cases where the original seller was not clear about it.
Do you buy loose diamonds that are not set in jewelry?
Yes. Loose stones are actually easier to grade accurately since nothing is hidden by a setting. Bring them in however you have them stored.
Do I need the original receipt or box to sell a diamond?
No. Paperwork can help establish provenance on branded pieces like Tiffany or Cartier, but it is not required to sell. Bring a valid government-issued ID, which New York law requires for this kind of transaction.
Can I sell a diamond I inherited?
Yes, inherited jewelry is very common here. You do not need paperwork tracing it back, just a valid ID of your own. If it is part of a larger estate, text photos ahead so we can set aside enough time.
How fast do I get paid for a diamond?
Same day. Once you accept the offer, you are paid before you leave the counter, with no waiting on a wire or a check to clear.
Is it better to sell a diamond in person or online?
Online buyers require you to mail your ring away and wait for an offer from someone you cannot see, and a lowered counteroffer often arrives once they already have your stone. In person you keep possession the entire time and can walk away at any point.
Do I need an appointment to sell a diamond?
Walk-ins are welcome during store hours. For a high-value stone or a full collection, a quick text to 917-608-8939 ahead of time helps us set aside proper time for it.
Are you licensed to buy diamonds in NYC?
Yes. NYC DCA license #2070477, Vintage USA Inc, DBA East Village Buyers.
