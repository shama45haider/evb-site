Before You Weigh

Sort It the Way a Buyer Will

Group by stamp
Each karat is priced at its own purity, so weigh 10K, 14K and 18K separately. Our stamp guide shows where to find the mark and what it means.
Leave out what isn’t gold
Stones, pearls, beads and steel parts don’t count. Pieces stamped GF, GP, HGE or RGP are plated or filled and hold very little gold.
Unmarked pieces
No stamp doesn’t mean no gold, and a stamp doesn’t prove it. Leave unmarked pieces out of your estimate. They get tested at the counter.
Dental gold
Crowns and bridges are often a high karat, but dental alloys vary. Leave them out of the calculator; they are tested on their own.
Kitchen scales
A kitchen scale gets you close, not exact. Here’s why, and why the certified scale at the counter settles it.

FAQ

Scrap Gold Questions

Quick answers about mixed and broken gold.

Can I add different karats together?
Add them as separate lines. Each karat holds a different share of gold, so a gram of 18K is worth more than a gram of 10K. The calculator prices each line at its own purity and adds them up.
What is a pennyweight?
An old jeweler’s unit: one pennyweight (dwt) is 1.555 grams. Some buyers quote in dwt, which makes offers hard to compare. The calculator takes either.
Does broken gold count the same as whole pieces?
Yes. Scrap is priced on the gold it contains, so a snapped chain is worth the same gram for gram as an intact one.
Do the stones count toward the weight?
No. Stones aren’t gold. They come out of the weight, and diamonds worth something on their own are valued separately.
Why is every offer below the melt value?
The buyer has to refine the gold, carry the risk of the price moving and run a business. Compare buyers on how close they come and whether they show you the weight, karat and spot.
