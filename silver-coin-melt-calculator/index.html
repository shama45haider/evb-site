Before You Count

Sort by Date First

1964 is the line
Dimes, quarters and halves dated 1964 or earlier are 90 percent silver. From 1965 they are copper-nickel, except Kennedy halves, which stayed 40 percent silver through 1970.
War nickels
From late 1942 through 1945, nickels were 35 percent silver. They have a large P, D or S mintmark above Monticello’s dome on the back. Some 1942 nickels are silver and some aren’t, so look for the mintmark.
Coins worth more than melt
Key dates such as the 1916-D Mercury dime or the 1932-D and 1932-S Washington quarters, Carson City Morgans and coins in high grade sell for more than their silver. Pull those out before counting the rest by face.
Cleaning
Don’t clean coins. It doesn’t change the silver and it can wipe out a collector premium.

FAQ

Silver Coin Questions

Quick answers about the silver in US coins.

How much silver is in a pre-1965 quarter?
0.1808 troy ounces as struck. Four of them, a dollar of face value, hold about 0.715 troy ounces once normal wear is counted.
Are 1965 quarters silver?
No. Dimes and quarters dated 1965 and later are copper-nickel and worth face value.
Which Kennedy halves are silver?
1964 Kennedy halves are 90 percent silver. 1965 through 1970 are 40 percent. Circulating halves from 1971 on have no silver.
How do I spot a war nickel?
Look for a large P, D or S mintmark above Monticello on the back of a 1942 to 1945 nickel. That placement was only used on the silver issues.
Why is it called junk silver?
It means common circulated coins valued for their silver rather than as collectibles. Our silver coins page explains how they’re counted and priced.
