How Bullion Is Priced

Priced Off Spot

Bullion is the most transparent thing we buy. There is no artistry to argue about and no collector premium to negotiate. There is a weight, a purity, and a live market price.

The calculation is weight multiplied by fineness multiplied by spot. A one-ounce .9999 bar contains one troy ounce of gold. A Krugerrand contains one troy ounce of gold despite weighing more than an ounce overall, because it is alloyed with copper for durability. That distinction — gross weight versus actual gold weight — is worth understanding before you compare offers anywhere.

Because it is this transparent, you can and should check spot on your phone while we work. We would rather you did.

Who Made It

Recognized Refiners Move Faster

Bars from well-known refiners are quicker to verify and therefore easier to transact on. PAMP Suisse, Credit Suisse, Valcambi, Perth Mint, the Royal Canadian Mint and Argor-Heraeus are all names we see constantly.

An unknown or unmarked bar is not a problem, it just takes longer. We test it properly rather than declining it, and the gold is worth what the gold is worth once we have confirmed the purity.

Counterfeit bars do exist, particularly tungsten-cored fakes in larger sizes, which is exactly why verification happens in front of you rather than in a back room.

Do Not Do This

Keep the Assay Card Sealed

Many smaller bars ship sealed in a tamper-evident assay card carrying a serial number that matches the bar.

Leave it sealed. An intact card and matching serial is documentary proof of weight and purity that transfers with the bar. Once you cut the bar out, that proof is gone, and while the gold has not changed, the bar becomes a piece of unverified metal that needs full testing.

It is a small difference on a one-ounce bar and a more meaningful one on larger sizes. People open them out of curiosity all the time and there is no way to put it back.

Coins Priced As Metal

Eagles, Krugerrands, Maple Leafs, Buffalos

Modern bullion coins are minted as investment products rather than as collectibles, so they are valued on gold content the same as bars.

American Gold Eagle — one troy ounce of gold, alloyed, so total weight is higher. Canadian Maple Leaf — .9999 fine. Krugerrand — one troy ounce of gold in a copper alloy. American Buffalo — .9999 fine. Fractional halves, quarters and tenths all trade too.

One exception worth knowing: a handful of specific years and special issues do carry collector premiums above bullion. If yours is one of them we will tell you, rather than quietly buying a scarce issue at melt.

If your coin is pre-1933 or clearly historic, it belongs on our collectible gold coins page instead, where the premium is the point.

Worth Considering

Why Not Mail It In?

Online bullion buyers quote attractive rates and the process sounds frictionless. The risk sits in the middle of it: you ship real money to a company you have never met, and the offer is finalised once they hold the metal.

Two things go wrong. Assay disputes, where the buyer claims the purity came in below expectation and you have no way to independently check. And dealer insolvency, which is not hypothetical — we have written about a case where customers' mailed-in metal was caught up in a bankruptcy.

Selling across a counter removes both. You hold the bar until you are paid, and if you do not like the number you put it back in your pocket and leave. For larger quantities, text ahead so we can have the cash ready.

FAQ

Gold Bullion Questions

Bullion is the most transparent thing we buy, so these are mostly about verification.

How much is a one-ounce gold bar worth today?
It tracks the live spot price of gold almost exactly, since bullion carries no design premium. The number moves throughout the day, so we price against live spot at the moment you are at the counter and you are welcome to check it yourself while we do.
Should I open the assay card on my gold bar?
No. Leave it sealed. An intact tamper-evident card with a matching serial number is proof of weight and purity that travels with the bar. Cutting it open does not change the gold but does remove that proof, which matters more on larger bars.
Do you buy bars from refiners I have never heard of?
Yes. A recognized refiner such as PAMP, Valcambi or the Perth Mint simply verifies faster. An unmarked or unfamiliar bar gets tested properly and is valued on confirmed purity.
Is a Krugerrand one ounce of gold?
It contains one troy ounce of gold but weighs more overall, because it is alloyed with copper for hardness. Gross weight and actual gold weight are different figures, and offers should always be compared on gold content.
Do you buy fractional bullion, like tenth-ounce coins?
Yes. Halves, quarters and tenth-ounce coins and small bars all trade. Smaller units carry a higher premium when bought new, which does not fully carry through on resale.
Can you handle a large bullion sale?
Yes, and a heads-up helps. Text 917-608-8939 before coming in with a substantial quantity so we can set aside the time and have the cash on hand.
Are some bullion coins worth more than spot?
A few specific years and special issues carry a collector premium above bullion value. If yours is one, we will tell you rather than buying a scarce issue at melt price.
Is it safer to sell bullion in person than by mail?
In person you keep the metal until you are paid and can walk away at any point. Mailing it means the offer is finalised while somebody else holds it, which exposes you to assay disputes and, in rare cases, to the dealer's own insolvency.
