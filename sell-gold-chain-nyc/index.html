Read the Stamp on the Clasp

Almost every real gold chain is stamped, usually on the clasp or on a small tag right beside it. You are looking for 10K, 14K, 18K or 22K, or the European equivalents: 417, 585, 750 and 916. Those numbers are the purity expressed in parts per thousand, so 585 means the metal is 58.5 percent gold.

A stamp on its own is not proof. Stamps get faked, and plated chains are often stamped to look convincing. That is why we acid-test and run an electronic reading at the counter regardless of what the tag claims.

Unstamped does not mean worthless either. Older chains and repaired pieces often lost their stamp along the way, and they test just fine.

The One That Surprises People

Hollow vs. Solid

This is where expectations and reality separate most often, so it is worth being blunt about it up front.

A great many rope chains, herringbones and larger Cuban links sold at retail are hollow — the links are formed from thin gold sheet wrapped around empty space. They look substantial and they wear well, but a hollow chain that looks like it should weigh 60 grams can come in at 12. You are paid for the gold that is actually there, and on a hollow chain there is far less of it than the size suggests.

How to tell before you come in: hollow chains feel unexpectedly light for their size, dent rather than bend, and often crimp permanently if crushed. A solid chain of the same length and width feels dense and drapes heavier in the hand.

None of this is a trick and it is not a lowball. A hollow chain was cheaper new than a solid one for exactly the same reason.

The Arithmetic

How the Number Is Built

Three inputs, no mystery: weight in grams, purity, and the live spot price of gold at the moment you are standing there.

A 14K chain is 58.5 percent gold, so a 30-gram 14K chain contains roughly 17.5 grams of pure gold. An 18K chain is 75 percent, so the same 30 grams contains about 22.5 grams pure. That purity difference is why two chains of identical weight can be worth meaningfully different amounts.

Spot moves every day. We price against it live rather than against a fixed in-house rate, and you are welcome to check the market on your phone while we weigh.

Styles

Cuban, Rope, Figaro, Box, Herringbone

The link style itself barely affects the melt number — gold is gold. Where style matters is in whether the chain is more valuable intact than melted, which happens with heavy solid Cuban links and well-made designer pieces that have resale demand of their own.

Condition matters less than people fear. A snapped link, a missing clasp, a kinked herringbone that will never lie flat again: none of that changes the gold content. Bring it in the state it is in.

One detail worth knowing: the clasp is frequently a different karat than the chain, sometimes a base metal entirely. We weigh and account for it separately rather than assuming.

The Honest Part

Plated, Filled and Vermeil

Gold-plated chains carry a microscopic layer of gold over brass or steel. There is effectively nothing to recover and we will tell you so rather than making an insulting offer for the sake of it.

Gold-filled is a thicker bonded layer and contains real gold, but the recoverable amount is small and often does not justify the trip on its own. Vermeil is gold over sterling silver, so the silver underneath carries the value rather than the plating.

If you genuinely do not know what you have, bring it. Testing is free, takes a minute, and you will leave knowing for certain either way.

FAQ

Gold Chain Questions, Answered Straight

Mostly about weight, because that is where nearly every surprise comes from.

Why is my gold chain worth less than I expected?
Nine times out of ten the chain is hollow rather than solid. Hollow rope and Cuban links are formed from thin gold wrapped around empty space, so a chain that looks like 60 grams can weigh 12. You are paid for the gold actually present, and a hollow chain cost less new for the same reason.
How can I tell if my chain is hollow or solid?
Hollow chains feel surprisingly light for their size, dent rather than bend, and can crimp permanently if crushed. A solid chain of the same width feels dense in the hand. If you are unsure, we will weigh and test it and tell you for certain.
What do the numbers 585 and 750 on my chain mean?
They are purity in parts per thousand. 585 is 14K, meaning 58.5 percent gold. 750 is 18K at 75 percent, 417 is 10K, and 916 is 22K. They are the European equivalent of the karat stamp.
My chain has no stamp. Is it still worth anything?
Very possibly. Older chains and repaired pieces often lose the stamp, and plenty of unstamped chains test as solid gold. We test rather than relying on the stamp anyway, since stamps can be faked.
Do you buy chains with a broken clasp or snapped links?
Yes, and the damage does not change the number. Gold content is what we pay for, and a broken link does not remove any gold. Bring it exactly as it is.
Is a Cuban link worth more than a rope chain?
Not because of the style itself, since gold is gold by weight. Heavy solid Cuban links can be worth more intact than melted if there is resale demand for the piece, and we will tell you when that is the case.
Do you buy gold-filled or gold-plated chains?
Plated chains have almost no recoverable gold and we will say so plainly. Gold-filled contains a real but small amount. Testing is free either way, so bring it if you are unsure.
Is the clasp the same gold as the chain?
Often not. Clasps are frequently a lower karat than the chain and occasionally base metal entirely. We weigh and account for the clasp separately rather than assuming it matches.
