Time, Risk, Effort

What the Fee Table Doesn't Show

The percentage is only part of the cost of selling online. Here is the rest.

Waiting for a buyer
Your pair sells when a buyer meets your price. A popular size can go fast; a slow one can sit, and the market price can move while it does.
Shipping deadlines
Once it sells, StockX gives you 2 business days to ship and GOAT gives you 3. Miss it and the order can be canceled, with a $15 penalty on StockX or $10 on GOAT.
Verification risk
Both platforms inspect the pair before you're paid. If it fails, the buyer is refunded and you can owe a fee. On GOAT, flaws found at inspection, like scuffs or missing laces, lead to a request to lower your price.
Condition limits
StockX's Verified Marketplace takes only brand-new items. Worn pairs need GOAT (with photos), StockX's Listings Marketplace, which is open only to select sellers for now, or a local buyer.
Days, not minutes
The money arrives after shipping, transit, inspection and a payout cycle, and on GOAT, a bank transfer after you cash out.
Selling at the counter
We check the pair in front of you, make an offer and pay the same day if you take it. No fees come off, nothing ships, and worn pairs are fine. You can also trade toward something in the store.

Streetwear

What About Grailed and Worn Pieces?

For streetwear, Grailed is the usual online option. It's peer-to-peer: you list the piece, set the price and ship it to the buyer. As published in November 2026, Grailed's seller fee is 9% on sales of $120 or more and 6% under $120 (minimum $1.99). Payment processing adds 3.49% plus $0.49 on US payments for sellers onboarded with Stripe. On a sale over $120, that's about 12.5% plus 49 cents before your shipping cost.

StockX also has a Listings Marketplace for used items. At launch it charges sellers zero fees, but selling access is limited to select US sellers.

On worn pieces, condition moves the price more than the platform does. Read how stains, fading and print cracking and a missing or damaged box change what buyers pay, wherever you sell.

The Honest Answer

When StockX or GOAT Is the Better Call

If the pair is new and you can wait, the platforms usually put more money in your pocket. We'd rather tell you that than pretend otherwise.

  • New and unworn, in the original box, in a size that sells steadily
  • You can wait a week or more for the money
  • You're fine packing, shipping on a deadline and handling a verification problem if one comes up
  • The gap between the online net and a cash offer is worth that time to you

Sell Locally

When Selling Locally Makes More Sense

For some sellers, cash today beats a bigger number later.

  • The pair is worn, creased, yellowed or missing its box
  • You need the money today, not after verification
  • You don't want shipping deadlines, penalties or a failed inspection
  • You're selling several pairs, or sneakers and streetwear together
  • You want to trade toward something in our store

FAQ

StockX, GOAT and Selling Locally

What sellers ask us before they decide where to sell.

Does StockX or GOAT pay more than selling locally?
Usually, for new pairs that sell steadily. As published in November 2026, a $300 sale nets about $259 on either platform after fees and shipping. A local cash offer is usually lower. Selling locally wins on speed, certainty and no fees, and it works for worn pairs that StockX's main marketplace won't take.
Can I sell worn sneakers on StockX?
Not on its Verified Marketplace, which takes only brand-new items. StockX's Listings Marketplace accepts used items, but selling access is limited to select US sellers for now. GOAT accepts used pairs with photos. We buy worn pairs at the counter.
How long does it take to get paid on StockX or GOAT?
Both pay after your pair ships, arrives and passes verification. StockX adds it to that evening's daily payout. GOAT adds it to your balance, and your bank takes 3 to 5 business days after you cash out. We pay the same day.
What happens if my pair fails verification?
The order is canceled and the buyer is refunded. StockX may return or keep the item and apply a verification-failure fee. GOAT charges $15 for an item it deems a replica, plus a fee to send it back. For flaws like scuffs or missing laces, GOAT asks you to accept a lower price.
Will a local shop pay what a pair sells for on StockX?
Usually not. The price on StockX or GOAT is what a buyer pays before your fees come off, and a shop buying for cash has to resell the pair itself. Use the net from the worked example above to decide whether same-day cash is worth the difference to you.
How do I get a quote from you?
