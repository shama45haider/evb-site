Top 5 Places to Sell an Engagement Ring in NYC

An engagement ring's value lives in three places: the center stone, the side stones, and the gold or platinum in the setting. Most of it usually sits in the center stone. Whoever you sell to should be able to show you how each part was priced.

Expect the number to be well below what the ring cost at retail. That gap comes from retail markup, and every buyer on this list works from resale prices. With that in mind, here are the five best places to sell an engagement ring in New York, ranked by speed, payout and what you give up to get it.

#2

Worthy

Online Auction · New York-Based

Worthy is a New York company that sells jewelry through online auctions to professional buyers. You describe the ring, then ship it fully insured by FedEx to its New York office, where it gets a grading report from partner gemologists. You set a reserve price with their team, the auction typically runs about 48 hours, and Worthy's fee is a percentage of the final price. Competing bids can help on a larger, well-graded stone. The trade-off is time, and the ring is out of your hands the whole way through.

Best for
Larger stones where competing bids can help.
Trade-off
Shipping, a wait, and a fee off the final price.

myGemma

Luxury Jewelry Buyer · NYC, by Appointment

myGemma buys and consigns designer jewelry and diamond rings, with in-person appointments at its New York office or insured shipping. #4

The Diamond District (West 47th Street)

Independent Buyers · Midtown

The Diamond District on West 47th Street is the center of New York's diamond trade, and plenty of its dealers buy from the public. You can collect several offers in an afternoon. Offers vary widely and some buyers only want the stone, so ask for the center stone and the setting to be priced separately. Don't leave a ring with anyone for a later answer.

#5

Louped, the jewelry marketplace that started out as I Do Now I Don't, gives you two routes: take a direct offer from the company, or list the ring at your own price for online buyers. When a ring sells to a buyer, it's checked by gemologists before the seller is paid. Listing it yourself can get closer to retail, but it takes longer and you handle buyer questions by message.

What Decides What a Ring Is Worth

  • The center stone. Graded on its own merits: cut, color, clarity and carat weight. A GIA report settles the grading question; here's what a GIA certificate proves.
  • Natural or lab-grown. Lab-grown stones resell for a fraction of a comparable natural diamond. More in lab-grown vs. natural resale.
  • Side stones. Pavé, halos and channel-set baguettes are valued as a group by total weight.
  • The setting. Gold or platinum, worth its metal content. A platinum head on a white gold band is common, and the two get weighed separately.
  • The brand. A ring from a house like Tiffany or Cartier can carry a premium on resale.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much can I get for my engagement ring?

It depends mostly on the center stone: its carat weight, cut, color, clarity and whether it's natural or lab-grown. Side stones and the metal in the setting add to that. Resale is well below retail, so text photos to 917-608-8939 for a realistic number.

Do I need a GIA certificate to sell my ring?

No. A GIA or IGI report speeds things up because the grading is already settled, but we grade the stone ourselves if there isn't one. Some buyers, such as myGemma, only take certain rings with a GIA report.

Will the buyer ask why I'm selling?

Not at East Village Buyers. Rings come across our counter after divorces, breakups, upgrades and estates, and none of it changes the number.

Can I sell a ring with a lab-grown diamond?

Yes, but expect a much lower number than for a natural stone, because lab-grown diamonds resell for a fraction of a comparable natural diamond. The gold or platinum setting still has its full metal value.

Should I sell the diamond and the setting separately?

You don't have to. We price the center stone, the side stones and the setting separately and show you each number, so you can see where the value is before you decide.
