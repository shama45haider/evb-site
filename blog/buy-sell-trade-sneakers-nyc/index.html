Where to Buy, Sell & Trade Sneakers in NYC (2026)

New York is the best city in the country to buy, sell and trade sneakers, and one of the easiest places to take a bad deal. Some shops pay cash on the spot. Some take your pairs on consignment and pay you after they sell. Some only sell. And a few of the most famous names have closed in the last two years, so plenty of older guides still send you to empty storefronts.

We're East Village Buyers, a buy, sell and trade shop at 39 Avenue A. We're on this list, and so are the places we'd point you to when they're the better fit. We checked every shop below in October 2026: what it actually does, where it is, and who it's best for.

Quick answer

Want cash today? Walk into East Village Buyers on Avenue A or District One on Stanton Street. Both look your pairs over and pay the same day.

Want the highest price and can wait? Consign with Flight Club, YankeeKicks or Stadium Goods. You're paid after the pair sells, minus their fees.

Want to swap pairs? East Village Buyers and Kickclusive both take trades.

Only buying? Kith, Extra Butter, Concepts and Undefeated get the best new releases, and StockX's SoHo store keeps about 300 deadstock pieces on hand.

Cash, Consignment or Trade: How Selling Sneakers Works in NYC

Every shop that takes sneakers from the public uses one of three models. Knowing which one you're walking into is the single biggest factor in what you walk out with.

ModelHow it worksWhen you get paidBest for
Sell for cashThe shop authenticates your pair and buys it outright. The offer sits below resale because the shop carries the risk of reselling it.Same visitUsed pairs, quick money, no fees or waiting
ConsignmentThe shop lists your pair at an agreed price and keeps a commission when it sells.After it sells, which can take weeksDeadstock and rare pairs worth the wait
TradeYou swap your pair, with cash on top either way if needed, for something in the shop's inventory.Right away, in productMoving into a pair you actually want

None of these is always best. A beat pair of Jordan 1s will rarely clear a consignment shop's standards, so selling for cash is the realistic route. A deadstock collab with a clean box might be worth consigning if you don't need the money this month. Plenty of sellers do both: consign the grails, sell the rest for cash.

How to Get the Best Offer on Your Sneakers

Whether you sell for cash, consign or trade, every serious shop authenticates first: stitching, materials, the size tag, the box label and how the pair glows under UV light. After that, the offer comes down to condition and completeness. A few things reliably push it up:

  • Bring the original box and everything that came with it. Spare laces, tags and the receipt all count. Here's how box condition and accessories affect resale value.
  • Clean them, but don't over-restore. A wipe-down helps. Aggressive sole bleaching or repainting can hurt. Yellowed soles aren't always a deal-breaker: here's when they matter.
  • Know the market before you walk in. Look up recent sales of your exact pair and size on StockX, GOAT or eBay so you know where an offer sits.
  • Get a cash offer and a consignment estimate side by side. Seeing the cash today next to a possible price in a few weeks makes the choice easy.
  • Bring a valid government-issued photo ID. New York law requires it when a secondhand dealer buys from you.
  • Sell more than one thing in one trip. At East Village Buyers you can sell sneakers, streetwear, gold and electronics in a single visit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I sell sneakers for cash in NYC today?

In Manhattan, East Village Buyers at 39 Avenue A and District One NY on Stanton Street both authenticate your pairs and pay the same day. In Brooklyn, Ron's Buy Sell Trade in Bushwick does the same. Bring a valid government-issued photo ID.

Is it better to consign sneakers or sell them for cash?

Consignment usually nets more on deadstock or rare pairs, but you wait until the pair sells and pay a commission. Selling for cash pays less than full resale but is instant and fee-free, which makes it the better route for worn pairs or when you need the money now.

Can I trade sneakers in NYC?

Yes. At East Village Buyers you can trade your pairs toward anything in our inventory, with cash on top either way if the values differ. Kickclusive in NoHo also buys and trades sneakers.

Do NYC sneaker shops buy used sneakers?

Some do. East Village Buyers and District One (114 Stanton St) both buy worn pairs. Consignment shops tend to focus on deadstock or near-new pairs.

Does Stadium Goods still have a store in NYC?

No. The Stadium Goods store at 47 Howard St closed in January 2025. Its Market Center at 412 Broadway is a drop-off point for consignors only, with nothing to shop.

What should I bring when I sell my sneakers?

Bring the original box, spare laces, tags and any receipt, plus a valid government-issued photo ID, which New York requires for secondhand sales. Clean the pairs lightly, but skip heavy restoration.
