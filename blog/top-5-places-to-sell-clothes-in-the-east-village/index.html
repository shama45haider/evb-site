Top 5 Places to Sell Clothes in the East Village

The East Village and the blocks around it are full of resale shops, and most of them will look at what you bring in. They don't all want the same things. A Supreme hoodie, a Zara coat, a Comme des Garçons jacket and a Moncler puffer each have a different best buyer, and walking into the wrong shop means a pass or a low number.

Here are the five best places to sell clothes in and around the East Village, what each one is looking for, and how you get paid.

#2

Buffalo Exchange

Buy-Sell-Trade · 332 East 11th Street

Buffalo Exchange's East Village store, on East 11th Street between First and Second Avenues, buys clothing and accessories any time it's open. It takes a wide range of labels, fast fashion included, and its buyers choose what fits the season and the store. You pick cash or trade credit, and trade is worth more. Bring clean, folded pieces and expect some of them to come back to you.

Best for
Everyday brands and current styles.
Trade-off
Picky about season and condition, and cash pays less than trade.

#3

2nd STREET

Japanese Resale Chain · 180 Orchard Street, Lower East Side

2nd STREET, the Japanese resale chain, buys and sells at its Lower East Side store on Orchard Street, a short walk south of the East Village. Its racks mix luxury, streetwear and vintage with sneakers, bags and accessories, so it takes a broader range than a typical thrift buyer. Offers are made in the store.

Best for
A mixed pile of clothing, shoes and accessories.
Trade-off
Like any buy counter, it pays a fraction of the rack price.
#4

Beacon's Closet

Buy-Sell-Trade · 10 West 13th Street

Beacon's Closet, a New York buy-sell-trade shop with three stores in Brooklyn, has its Manhattan location on West 13th Street between Fifth and Sixth Avenues, a short trip west of the East Village. It buys gently used, in-season clothing, shoes, jewelry and accessories, and pays in cash or a larger amount in store credit.

Best for
Gently used, in-season clothing and shoes.
Trade-off
Store credit pays more than cash.
#5

Tokio7

Designer Consignment · 83 East 7th Street

Tokio7 has been selling designer and vintage clothing on consignment in the East Village since the 1990s, and it's known for Japanese and avant-garde labels such as Comme des Garçons, Yohji Yamamoto and Rick Owens. It takes in-store consignment by appointment, so you're paid after your pieces sell rather than on the spot. For high-end designer pieces that need the right buyer, it's a natural fit.

Best for
Designer pieces that need the right buyer.
Trade-off
Consignment, so you wait for a sale, and it's by appointment.

How to Get More for Your Clothes

  • Wash and fold everything. Clean, fresh pieces get offers. A wrinkled bag of laundry gets passed over.
  • Keep the inside labels. Removed tags and altered collars cut resale value on streetwear. More in our guide to tags and collars.
  • Check prints and fabric. Cracked prints, fading and stains lower the number. See how condition is judged.
  • Sell in season. Coats and puffers sell best going into winter, shorts and tees going into summer.
  • Split your pile. Bring streetwear and designer to a specialist and everyday brands to a general buy-sell-trade shop. A walk up Avenue A and over to East 11th Street covers both.

Which Option Is Right for You?

  • Streetwear, sneakers or designer, for cash today: East Village Buyers at 39 Avenue A.
  • Everyday brands in good shape: Buffalo Exchange or Beacon's Closet.
  • A mixed pile with some luxury in it: 2nd STREET.
  • High-end designer you're willing to wait on: Tokio7.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I sell clothes for cash in the East Village?

East Village Buyers at 39 Avenue A pays cash the same visit for streetwear, sneakers and designer clothing. Buffalo Exchange on East 11th Street buys everyday brands for cash or trade credit.

What clothes sell for the most in the East Village?

Streetwear and designer labels with strong resale demand, such as Supreme, BAPE, Chrome Hearts, Hellstar and Moncler, bring the highest offers. Everyday brands sell too, but for much less, and mostly when they're current and in season.

Is it better to take cash or store credit?

At shops like Buffalo Exchange and Beacon's Closet, store credit is worth more than cash, so it makes sense if you plan to shop there. If you want money in hand, take cash. East Village Buyers pays cash, or you can trade toward something in the store.

Can I sell clothes without the tags?

Yes. Retail hang tags aren't needed. Inside labels and wash tags are different: on streetwear they're part of how a piece is authenticated, so don't cut them out.
