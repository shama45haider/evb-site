Where to Buy, Sell & Trade Pokémon Cards in NYC (2026)

Pokémon cards are a real market now. A single card can be worth more than a used car, and sealed boxes from a few years ago can sell for several times what they cost. That has made New York a busy place to buy, sell and trade, with new card shops, trade nights and weekend shows. It has also filled search results with spam pages that list shops that don't exist.

We're East Village Buyers, a buy, sell and trade shop at 39 Avenue A, and Pokémon cards and sealed product are part of what we buy every week. Every shop and event below was checked in October 2026: where it is, what it does with Pokémon, and who it's best for.

Want to play? The Trainer Court runs Pokémon League, and Project TCG in Flushing hosts League Cups.

How Card Shops Price Pokémon Cards

Before you sell anywhere, it helps to know how the offer is built. Nearly every shop starts from a market price, usually TCGplayer's, which is based on recent completed sales of the same card in the same condition and printing. It then offers a percentage of that number, through what shops call a buylist.

  • Cash usually pays less than store credit. Credit costs the shop less than cash, so most pay more in credit. The Trainer Court, for example, publishes up to 70% in cash and up to 80% in credit on sealed product.
  • Condition is everything. Shops buy modern singles in Near Mint or Lightly Played condition. Whitening on the edges, creases or scratches on the holo drop the price fast.
  • Low-value cards are hard to sell. Bulk commons and cards worth a few dollars rarely justify a shop's time. Bleecker Trading focuses on cards worth $30 or more, and The Trainer Court has paused buying bulk.
  • Market prices can mislead on vintage. Rare vintage cards trade so rarely that the last sale may be weeks old. That's why shops price them case by case.

Should You Grade Your Pokémon Cards Before Selling?

Grading puts a card in a sealed slab with a 1-to-10 score from a company like PSA, CGC or BGS. A high grade on the right card can multiply its value. It's not worth it for every card, though:

  • Grade only cards that are valuable and close to perfect. A PSA 10 needs near-perfect centering, corners, edges and surface. A card that would come back a 7 often sells for less than the grading cost plus your time.
  • Expect a wait. PSA has paused its cheapest tiers this fall because of a backlog of millions of cards. Check psacard.com for current tiers and turnaround times before you submit.
  • Sealed is simpler. Booster boxes and Elite Trainer Boxes don't need grading. Keep the shrink wrap intact, because opened or resealed product is worth far less.

How to Get the Best Offer on Your Pokémon Cards

  • Sleeve and toploader your best cards. Loose cards rubbing together in a box lose condition, and condition is the price.
  • Sort before you go. Separate the cards worth real money from bulk. A shop can price a short stack of hits quickly and fairly.
  • Look up recent sales yourself. Check TCGplayer and eBay sold listings for your exact card, set and condition, so you know what a fair offer looks like.
  • Get two offers on anything big. A cash offer at a shop and a few offers at a show make it obvious where the market is.
  • Bring a valid government-issued photo ID. New York requires secondhand dealers to record who they buy from.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I sell Pokémon cards for cash in NYC?

East Village Buyers at 39 Avenue A pays cash the same day for sealed Pokémon product, graded slabs and vintage cards. The Trainer Court in the Meatpacking District buys through a buylist, paying store credit or PayPal within two weeks, and Bleecker Trading buys higher-value and graded cards. Bring a valid government-issued photo ID.

Where can I trade Pokémon cards in NYC?

Trade nights at Bleecker Trading and CardVault by Tom Brady, and card shows like the New York Evolving TCG Show in Flushing, are the easiest places to trade. Project TCG in Flushing also buys, sells and trades in store.

How much will a card shop pay for my Pokémon cards?

Shops pay a percentage of the card's market price, usually TCGplayer's, and pay more in store credit than in cash. The Trainer Court, for example, publishes up to 70% in cash and 80% in credit on sealed product. Condition, demand and how quickly the shop can resell all affect the offer.

Can I sell bulk Pokémon cards in NYC?

It is harder than selling individual valuable cards. Many shops focus on cards worth $30 or more, and some have paused buying bulk. Sort out your valuable cards first and sell those individually.

Where can I get Pokémon cards graded in NYC?

The Trainer Court is an official PSA dealer and takes in-person Pokémon submissions, and CardVault says it offers grading submissions. PSA's cheapest tiers have been paused this fall because of a backlog, so check current tiers and turnaround times first.

Is there a Pokémon Center store in NYC?

No. The Pokémon Center store in New York closed in 2005. Today the city's Pokémon buying, selling and trading happens at independent card shops, trade nights and card shows.
